About the journal

WIJEAS publishes original research across the engineering disciplines and the applied sciences that underpin them. It brings computing, civil, mechanical, electrical and bio-engineering work into one venue, on a single condition: every contribution must advance an engineered solution, method, system or design.

Vision

To be a trusted international home for engineering research that turns scientific insight into working solutions for society.

Mission

To publish rigorous, reproducible and application-oriented research across all branches of engineering and the applied sciences, reviewed fairly by international experts and freely available to every reader.

Goals

  1. Publish four issues every year, on schedule in March, June, September and December, each with at least five peer-reviewed articles.
  2. Maintain an active editorial board of recognised scholars from several countries, every member listed with full affiliation and ORCID iD.
  3. Keep peer review fair, transparent and timely, with a first decision targeted within eight weeks.
  4. Meet in full the evaluation criteria of leading international indexing and abstracting services, and of the UGC Suggestive Parameters.
  5. Build subject editorial teams covering computing and AI, civil, mechanical, electrical and bio-engineering.
  6. Grow the share of articles that report industrial application, field validation or open datasets and code.

Core values

Integrity

Honest, ethical research and publishing at every stage.

Rigour

Decisions based on sound method and evidence.

Openness

Free access to knowledge for every reader, everywhere.

Fairness

Impartial review, independent of fees, affiliation or nationality.

Inclusion

A platform for researchers of every country, institution and career stage.

Readership

Researchers, faculty and doctoral scholars in engineering and the applied sciences, industrial R&D teams, and technology practitioners.

What distinguishes the journal

  • A clearly bounded scope: the journal publishes only research within its stated aims and declines work that belongs elsewhere, so readers and authors know exactly what it stands for.
  • Double-anonymous peer review by at least two independent experts, with editorial decisions made on scholarly merit alone.
  • Full open access under CC BY 4.0 at no cost to readers, with authors retaining copyright.
  • Adherence to COPE Core Practices, discipline-specific reporting standards and transparent policies on ethics, data and generative AI.
  • Permanent identifiers and preservation: Crossref DOIs for every article, ORCID iDs for authors, and long-term digital archiving.

Aims and scope

WAU International Journal of Engineering and Applied Sciences publishes original research in engineering and the applied sciences.

Aims

  • Publish rigorous, reproducible engineering research that advances methods, systems and applications.
  • Provide a single, credible venue for work that crosses engineering branches, such as AI in structural monitoring or energy systems for medical devices.
  • Connect academic research with industrial practice through application-focused articles, case studies and technical notes.
  • Hold every article to international standards of peer review, reporting and research integrity, whatever the author's country or career stage.

Scope

The journal welcomes submissions in the following areas. Each area is handled by its own Associate Editor.

Out of scope

To keep the scope clear and to direct each manuscript to the most suitable venue, the journal does not consider:

  • Purely theoretical science with no engineering realisation.
  • Security, cryptography and digital forensics research, which is directed to WAU International Journal of Cyber Security, Cyber Crime and Cyber Psychology.
  • Health technology where the contribution is clinical rather than engineering, which is directed to WAU International Journal of Health and Medical Sciences.

Manuscripts outside the scope are returned at initial screening, usually within 7 days, without peer review, so that authors can submit elsewhere promptly.

Board composition policy

  • The board draws members from several countries and institutions. No single institution, including the publisher, will account for more than a small minority of the board.
  • Every member is listed with full name, institutional affiliation, country and ORCID iD, and has consented to appointment.
  • Members are expected to handle manuscripts, review in their field and advise on journal strategy. Inactive members are replaced at the annual board review.
  • Articles authored by editorial board members, including the Editor-in-Chief, are handled entirely by an independent editor with no conflict of interest, and such articles will not exceed 10% of the content of any volume.

Author guidelines

Before you submit

  • Confirm that the manuscript fits the aims and scope.
  • Confirm that it is original, not published and not under consideration elsewhere.
  • Obtain ethics approval and consent where required, and prepare the relevant reporting checklist.

Manuscript preparation

  • Language. English, in clear academic style. Authors may use professional language editing; this does not guarantee acceptance.
  • File format. Microsoft Word (.docx) or LaTeX, with figures in high resolution (at least 300 dpi).
  • Anonymisation. Submit a separate title page. Remove author names, affiliations, acknowledgements and self-identifying references from the anonymised manuscript.

Title page (separate file)

  • Concise, informative title (up to 20 words).
  • Full names, affiliations, countries, emails and ORCID iDs of all authors.
  • Corresponding author details.
  • Affiliations in full: department, institution, city and country for every author. Authors without an institutional affiliation should state "Independent Researcher" with city and country.
  • Funding: the name of each funder and the grant or award number, or the statement that the research received no specific grant from any funding agency.
  • Funding statement, competing interests statement, CRediT author contributions, acknowledgements, ethics statement and data availability statement.

Anonymised manuscript

  • Abstract. Up to 250 words. Research articles use a structured abstract: Background or Purpose; Methods or Approach; Findings; Conclusions or Implications. The abstract must stand alone and contain no citations.
  • Keywords. Four to six keywords that do not repeat the title.
  • Main text. Introduction; Literature Review (where appropriate); Methods; Results; Discussion; Limitations; Conclusion. Theoretical articles may use a structure suited to the argument.
  • Tables and figures. Numbered consecutively, cited in the text, with self-explanatory titles and legends.
  • References. IEEE reference style (numbered, in order of citation). Every in-text citation must appear in the reference list and every reference must be cited. Include DOIs for all references that have them. Cite primary, peer-reviewed sources wherever possible.

Article types

Word limits exclude the title page, abstract, references, tables and figure legends. The editors may allow longer articles where justified.

Original Research ArticleFull report of original empirical, experimental or theoretical research. Up to 8,000 words.
Review ArticleSystematic, scoping, narrative or critical review that synthesises a body of research and sets out future directions. Up to 9,000 words.
Short CommunicationConcise report of novel findings of particular importance. Up to 3,000 words.
Technical NoteA concise report of a new method, device, algorithm or significant improvement. Up to 4,000 words.
Industrial Case StudyA documented application of engineering methods in a real industrial or infrastructure setting, with measured outcomes. Up to 6,000 words.
Data or Software ArticleDescribes a dataset or research software of reuse value, deposited in a recognised open repository. Up to 3,000 words.
EditorialCommissioned by the Editor-in-Chief. Not peer reviewed; clearly labelled.
Book ReviewCritical review of a recent scholarly book within scope. Up to 2,000 words.

All article types except Editorials are peer reviewed. Every article type is published under the same open access licence and receives a DOI.

Reporting standards

  • Describe the experimental set-up, materials, parameters, instrumentation and measurement uncertainty in sufficient detail for replication.
  • Benchmark proposed methods against established baselines using standard datasets or test conditions, and report statistical variation across repeated runs.
  • For machine learning work, state dataset provenance and licence, train, validation and test splits, hyperparameters, compute resources and evaluation protocol.
  • Provide a code and data availability statement; deposit code in a public repository (for example GitHub with a Zenodo DOI) wherever possible.
  • Report units in SI and follow relevant national or international standards (for example BIS, IEC, ISO, ASTM) where they apply.

How review works

  1. Screening for scope, completeness, similarity and ethics, usually within 7 days.
  2. Assignment to the Associate Editor for the scope area.
  3. Double-anonymous review by at least two independent experts.
  4. Decision: accept, minor revision, major revision or reject. First decision targeted within 8 weeks.
  5. Copyediting, author proofs and online publication with a DOI.

These are targets, not guarantees. The journal never shortens review to meet a timeline.

Submission checklist

  • Manuscript is within scope and within the word limit for its article type.
  • Title page and anonymised manuscript are prepared as separate files.
  • Structured abstract and keywords are included.
  • Ethics approval, consent and any trial registration are stated.
  • Reporting guideline checklist is attached where applicable.
  • Funding, competing interests, CRediT contributions and data availability statements are complete.
  • References follow the journal style and include DOIs.
  • Permissions for third-party material have been obtained.
  • Any use of generative AI is disclosed.
  • A cover letter explains the contribution and confirms originality.

Special issues and conference papers

Special issues bring together high-quality research on a focused theme within the journal's scope. They follow exactly the same peer review and ethics standards as regular issues.

Proposing a special issue

  • Proposals are submitted to the Editor-in-Chief with the theme, rationale, relevance to scope, proposed guest editors with affiliations and ORCID iDs, and a timeline.
  • Guest editors must hold a doctorate or equivalent standing in the field and have a verifiable publication record. Their credentials and competing interests are checked before approval.
  • The journal normally publishes no more than one special issue per volume, and special issue articles are assigned to regular quarterly issues.

Peer review of special issue papers

  • Every paper is double-anonymous peer reviewed by at least two independent reviewers.
  • Final acceptance decisions are approved by the Editor-in-Chief or a designated regular editor, not by guest editors alone.
  • Papers authored by a guest editor are handled entirely by a regular editor, and guest editors may author no more than one article in their special issue.

Papers from conferences

Papers first presented at a conference, including conferences organised by the publisher, are considered only as full, substantially extended manuscripts. They must add significant new content, analysis or results beyond the conference version, disclose the earlier presentation, and undergo full peer review. Presentation at a conference never guarantees acceptance.

Reviewer guidelines

Peer reviewers are central to the quality of the journal. We are grateful to every reviewer who gives their time and expertise.

Before accepting an invitation

  • Accept only if the manuscript is within your expertise and you can report within 21 days.
  • Decline if you have a competing interest, including recent collaboration with, or supervision of, any author, or a direct commercial interest.

Writing the review

  • Summarise the manuscript and its main contribution.
  • Assess originality, significance, methodological rigour, adherence to reporting standards, ethics, interpretation of results and clarity.
  • Separate major concerns from minor points, and make specific, constructive suggestions.
  • Give separate confidential comments to the editor where needed, and a recommendation: accept, minor revision, major revision or reject.
  • Write courteously. Personal remarks are not acceptable.

Confidentiality

Manuscripts are confidential. Do not share them, contact the authors, use unpublished findings, or upload manuscripts or reports to generative AI tools.

Recognition

Reviewers may receive a review certificate and may record verified reviews on ORCID. The journal publishes an annual list thanking its reviewers. Reviewers can register at https://wijeas.in/reviewer-registration.

Standards and best practice

The journal's policies and workflows follow recognised international standards for scholarly publishing, and are designed to meet the evaluation criteria used by leading international indexing and abstracting services.

Standards the journal follows

  • COPE Core Practices. Publication ethics and handling of misconduct.
  • Principles of Transparency and Best Practice in Scholarly Publishing. Joint principles of COPE, DOAJ, OASPA and WAME, covering website content, peer review, ownership, fees and archiving.
  • Reporting guidelines. Discipline-specific reporting standards, including EQUATOR Network guidelines where relevant.
  • Open access. Budapest Open Access Initiative definition; Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence.
  • Persistent identifiers. Crossref DOIs for articles, ORCID iDs for authors, CRediT contributor roles.
  • Preservation. Long-term digital archiving.

Alignment with the UGC Suggestive Parameters

For researchers in India, the journal is designed in line with the eight criteria of the University Grants Commission's Suggestive Parameters for peer-reviewed journals: valid ISSN and regular quarterly publication; a qualified editorial board with verifiable affiliations; published editorial policies; quality criteria for published content; recognised publishing standards; research ethics; visibility through open access and indexing; and scholarly impact.

The UGC does not approve or list individual journals. Institutions assess journals against these parameters themselves.

Publication frequency

WAU International Journal of Engineering and Applied Sciences is a quarterly journal. It publishes one volume per calendar year, comprising four issues released in March, June, September and December. Each issue contains a minimum of five peer-reviewed articles.

Articles are published online with a DOI as soon as they have completed production ("Articles in press"), and are then assigned to the next scheduled issue. This keeps publication fast for authors and the quarterly schedule regular for readers and indexing services.
